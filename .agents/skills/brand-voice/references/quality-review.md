# Writing quality review

Use these checks on a draft or the passage under review. Read the whole passage for meaning first. Apply the checks
within the user's requested scope; a small edit needs a small review. Diagnose a problem before changing the text.

## Meaning, structure and voice

- Give each passage a useful job. Check whether the main point, decision or action is buried, context is missing,
  references are ambiguous, or steps depend on information introduced too late.
- Let the genre determine structure. An argument needs evidence and reasoning; instructions need usable steps;
  a narrative may need pacing or suspense. A clear technical explanation can be complete without a benefit pitch.
- Check register, emphasis and humor against the target voice established for the task. Investigate unintended shifts
  into corporate, academic, promotional or intimate language. A requested brand transformation should change how the
  draft sounds; preserve its meaning and factual uncertainty while making that change deliberate and consistent.
- Read neighboring sentences together. Vary length and structure around the information, rather than turning every
  sentence into a short declaration. Keep formatting that helps navigation and remove emphasis that competes with it.

## Evidence and specificity

Separate an objective claim from an opinion, value, intention or hypothetical possibility. Check that the wording
preserves its status and supported scope. A memorable expression must not quietly change what is being promised.

Use an available actor, action, mechanism, example, date or amount to replace vague praise. Numbers are useful when
supported, not compulsory. Never invent a metric, quote, testimonial, source, guarantee or personal experience to
make an edit seem more concrete. When the needed fact is absent, identify the gap or ask for it.

Check attribution, qualifications, causal links and the context of statistics. Mark claims needing verification
separately from wording suggestions. Removing an awkward sentence does not establish whether the underlying claim
is true. Preserve accurate specialist language, material conditions and uncertainty.

## Formulaic and inflated writing

Read [deslopify-writing](../../deslopify-writing/SKILL.md) for the exact phrase list, broader pattern catalog and
contextual examples. Use those checks alongside the chosen voice techniques. Replace empty expression with its
underlying fact rather than a synonym; keep literal and technical meanings and effective rhetorical choices.

## Preserve effective creative devices

Check what the device accomplishes before removing it. These judgments keep the voice techniques and the pattern
review working together:

- Keep "Different desks. One draft." when shared editing makes the contrast meaningful. Rework a contrast that
  merely announces a grander description of the same thing.
- Keep an accumulated list of imagined workshop projects when it helps the reader picture a possibility. Rework
  a decorative list of interchangeable aspirations. The number of items alone does not decide.
- Keep a genuine FAQ or an unanswered question that invites useful reflection. Remove a repeated question-and-answer
  setup that delays information the reader needs.
- Keep humor that carries a supported insight, and emotional language grounded in the subject. Remove borrowed
  bravado, false vulnerability, invented intimacy or an inflated conclusion unsupported by the piece.

After cutting formulaic writing, read the piece again. It should express the intended personality, cadence and emphasis; artificial
mistakes, slang, invented anecdotes and uniformly clipped sentences do not make it more human.

## For marketing and conversion copy

Use the relevant dimensions from copy-editing's focused sweeps. Keep the work proportional to the request; do not
require seven separate reports or repeat every earlier pass after each edit.

- **Reader benefit:** connect an important capability to a consequence this audience cares about when supported.
  Not every sentence needs a "which means" clause, and a feature can be the right answer for a technical reader.
- **Proof:** support consequential claims with available evidence. Request missing proof or narrow the claim;
  testimonials, customer logos and endorsements require actual material and permission where applicable.
- **Specificity:** show the relevant mechanism or example. Invented precision is worse than an honest qualification.
- **Emotion:** use a relevant worry, aspiration, relief or delight when the purpose calls for it. Informational copy
  need not become emotional, and uncertainty must not become fear or manufactured urgency.
- **Action and objections:** make the next step and its consequences understandable. Address relevant price,
  commitment, setup, cancellation or privacy questions with known facts. Add a free trial, refund promise or risk
  reversal only when it is a real part of the offer; do not describe an action as risk-free by default.

For content refreshes, identify wording tied to old dates, product behavior, prices, links or evidence. Read the
available current material or flag what needs verification.

## For documents an agent consumes

Use [writing-for-agents](../../writing-for-agents/SKILL.md) when the task is a skill, agent instruction file or
guidance an agent will follow. Give instructions a clear scope and observable completion criteria. Keep shared rules
in one authoritative place; reveal task-specific detail through references that state when to read them.

Prefer a concrete action and outcome over motivational prose. Distinguish requirements from examples, optional
advice and supplied data. Apply the writing checks to explanations without adding a campaign persona to operational
instructions.
