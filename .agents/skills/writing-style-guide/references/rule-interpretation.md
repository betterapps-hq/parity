# Rule interpretation and overlaps

Apply a prescription to the content it describes. A rule for a UI label does not automatically govern a paragraph,
and a translation constraint does not automatically govern a campaign in one language. Retain words such as
"usually," "in general," "unless" and "when space is limited" when applying a rule.

## Competing entries in the detailed reference

The collected articles include older general entries and newer or more specific prescriptions. Their original
qualifications and examples remain in the reference. The following decisions resolve known conflicts for use:

| Topic | Working rule | Detailed entries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Percentages | Use a numeral followed immediately by `%`. Use "percentage" for an unspecified proportion. Don't begin a sentence with a percent sign or use it as the name of the symbol. | [Percent, percentage](terms-p.md#percent-percentage) and [numbers checklist](checklists.md#numbers-checklist). The older general Numbers example spells out "percent". |
| Negative numbers and subtraction | Use the minus sign `−`, not an en dash or hyphen. | [Dashes, hyphens and minus signs](punctuation.md#em-dashes-en-dashes-hyphens-and-minus-signs). The older general Numbers example uses an en dash. |
| Spelled-out measurement units | Use singular for exactly 1 and plural for all other quantities, including 0 and decimal fractions. | [Units of measure](terms-collections.md#units-of-measure-terms) and [Numbers](numbers.md#numbers). The numbers checklist contains a conflicting singular-at-one-or-less prescription. |
| Short list items and periods | Headings and UI labels keep their placement-specific treatment. For prose lists, follow the newer Periods entry: when one or more items are complete sentences, punctuate all items. When all items are short phrases of three words or fewer, omit periods, even if they complete the introduction. | [Periods](punctuation.md#periods) and [Lists](content-structure.md#lists). The Lists article contains a broader short-item exception. |
| Alt text and tooltip punctuation | Apply the element-specific entry. Alt text normally starts with a capital and ends with a period when practical. A tooltip fragment has no period; a full sentence does. | [Alt text](accessibility.md#alternative-text-alt-text) and [tooltip](terms-t.md#tooltip). Avoid extending the general rule for short UI labels to every text element. |

These are explicit interpretations of competing entries, not additional restrictions on unrelated work. If another
conflict affects the result and cannot be resolved by the content type, specificity or stated update, identify it in
the review rather than inventing a rule or silently dropping a qualification.

## Brand expression and practical product writing

- Brand expression can use "we" and "our" to speak directly for the organization. Product UI usually focuses on the
  reader and avoids a corporate "we"; it is appropriate where the speaker must be clear, such as privacy information.
  See [Person](grammar.md#person).
- An open-ended question can invite participation in a campaign. Practical product copy usually supplies an answer;
  a question is useful when a person must make a choice. See [Question marks](punctuation.md#question-marks).
- Unexpected vocabulary and metaphor can express a brand perspective. Operational instructions use familiar meanings
  and consistent terminology; worldwide or machine-translated content needs special care with idioms and wordplay.
- Parallel syntax in headings, term lists and procedures supports scanning. Mechanically repeating an empty rhetorical
  setup across paragraphs does not serve that function. Check meaning and placement rather than counting repetitions.
- Genuine hypotheticals and statements of values can be imaginative or unequivocal. Keep them distinguishable from
  claims about available capabilities, measured outcomes or guarantees.
